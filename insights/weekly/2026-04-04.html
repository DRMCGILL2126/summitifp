Weekly Market Recap April 04, 2026

Markets Rise Amid US Iran Tensions

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow Jones, and Russell 2000 indices posted significant gains of 3.8%, 4.8%, 2.9%, and 4.9%, respectively, despite escalating US-Iran tensions.
  • The technology sector led the market with a 6.7% gain, while energy was the biggest loser, plummeting 4.4% due to concerns about global oil supplies and Middle East conflict.
  • The US labor market reported its largest jobs gain in 15 months, but the ongoing Iran conflict continues to cast a shadow over the market, with investors taking a wait-and-see approach.
  • Industrials and real estate also had a strong week, rising 4.6% and 3.5%, respectively, as investors sought out sectors with limited exposure to the Middle East conflict.

As I reflect on the week's market action, it's clear that investors were focused on the escalating tensions between the US and Iran. Despite the geopolitical uncertainty, major indices managed to post significant gains, with the S&P 500 rising 3.8% and the Nasdaq surging 4.8%. The Dow Jones also had a strong week, up 2.9%, while the Russell 2000 led the charge with a 4.9% gain.

The news headlines were dominated by the Iran conflict, with reports of airstrikes, downed fighter jets, and rejected ceasefire proposals. However, it was the technology sector that stole the show, with a 6.7% gain, likely driven by the ongoing demand for innovative solutions in a rapidly changing world. Industrials and real estate also had a strong week, rising 4.6% and 3.5%, respectively. On the other hand, energy was the clear laggard, plummeting 4.4% as the conflict in the Middle East raised concerns about global oil supplies.

Three stories stood out to me as particularly influential in shaping the week's market action. Firstly, the reports of Iran allowing essential goods vessels to its ports via the Hormuz Strait helped to ease some concerns about global trade disruptions. Secondly, the news of India making its first Iranian oil purchase in seven years without payment problems suggested that some countries are finding ways to navigate the complex geopolitical landscape. Lastly, the US labor market's largest jobs gain in 15 months was a welcome surprise, but the ongoing Iran conflict and its potential impact on the global economy continue to cast a shadow over the market.

As I scanned the sector performance, it was clear that the conflict in the Middle East had a significant impact on the energy sector. The 4.4% decline in energy stocks was likely driven by concerns about supply chain disruptions and the potential for further escalation. On the other hand, the technology sector's strong performance was likely driven by the ongoing demand for innovative solutions, as well as the fact that many tech companies have limited exposure to the Middle East conflict.

In terms of company-specific moves, there weren't any major surprises, but the market's reaction to the geopolitical news was telling. The fact that investors were willing to bid up stocks despite the uncertainty suggests that many are taking a wait-and-see approach, hoping that the conflict will be resolved without major long-term consequences.
