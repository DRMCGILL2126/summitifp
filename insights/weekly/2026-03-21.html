Weekly Market Recap March 21, 2026

Global Markets Plunge Amid Rising Uncertainty

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq indices all declined by roughly 3% this week, with the S&P 500 Growth index falling 3.5% and the S&P 500 Value index declining 2.6%.
  • The Energy sector was a notable exception, rising 2.4% due to higher oil prices, while sectors like Consumer Staples, Materials, and Utilities were among the worst performers.
  • International markets were also affected, with the MSCI EAFE and MSCI Emerging Markets indices down 4.6% and 4.7%, respectively, amid escalating tensions in the Iran conflict.
  • President Trump's statement about potentially "winding down" the Iran war was seen as a potentially positive development, but its impact on the markets remains uncertain, with investors closely watching the situation and government responses.

This week was marked by significant declines across major indices, with the S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq all dropping by roughly 3%. The S&P 500 Growth index was particularly hard hit, falling 3.5%, while the S&P 500 Value index declined 2.6%. The international markets also felt the pinch, with the MSCI EAFE and MSCI Emerging Markets indices down 4.6% and 4.7%, respectively.

The driving force behind these declines was the ongoing conflict in Iran, which has led to increased uncertainty and volatility in the markets. News headlines this week were dominated by stories of escalating tensions, missile strikes, and efforts by various countries to mitigate the impact of the conflict on global energy supplies. As a result, energy prices have surged, and this has had a ripple effect on various sectors. The Energy sector was one of the few bright spots, rising 2.4% on the back of higher oil prices.

The biggest stories of the week were undoubtedly the escalation of the Iran conflict, the impact on global energy supplies, and the efforts by countries like the US and UK to respond to the situation. The news that the US is considering "winding down" the Iran war, as stated by President Trump, was seen as a potentially positive development, but it's unclear how this will play out in the coming weeks. The declaration of force majeure by Iraq on foreign-operated oilfields due to Hormuz disruption was also a significant development, highlighting the far-reaching consequences of the conflict.

In terms of sector performance, it's no surprise that Energy was the top performer, given the surge in oil prices. Financials also held up relatively well, declining just 0.5%. On the other hand, sectors like Consumer Staples, Materials, and Utilities were among the worst performers, likely due to concerns about the impact of higher energy prices on consumer spending and industrial production. Notable company-specific moves included United Airlines, which announced plans to cut more flights due to high oil prices, and Honeywell, which received an upgrade from some analysts.

As we look ahead to next week, it's clear that the situation in Iran will continue to be a major driver of market sentiment. Any further escalation of the conflict, or signs of de-escalation, will likely have a significant impact on energy prices and, in turn, the broader markets. Additionally, investors will be keeping a close eye on the response of governments and central banks to the crisis, as well as any potential implications for global economic growth.
