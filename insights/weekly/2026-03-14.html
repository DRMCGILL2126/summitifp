Weekly Market Recap March 14, 2026

Markets Decline Amid Global Economic Uncertainty

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq declined around 2-3% this week, while the energy sector rose 2.5% due to increasing oil prices caused by disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Geopolitical tensions, particularly the escalating conflict between the US and Iran, drove market volatility, with the financials sector falling 2.9% and the consumer discretionary sector dropping 3.3%.
  • The industrials and communication services sectors also struggled, falling 3.7% and 2.6% respectively, while the technology sector declined 2.1% due to concerns about the conflict's impact on global supply chains.
  • Barclays raised its 2026 Brent forecast to $85 a barrel, reflecting growing concerns about supply disruptions, and companies like ExxonMobil and Chevron likely benefited from the higher oil prices.

This week was a challenging one for markets, with all major indices declining. The S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq were each down around 2-3%, while the S&P MidCap 400 and Russell 2000 fell by 2.9% and 2.8%, respectively. The energy sector was the sole bright spot, rising 2.5% on the back of increasing oil prices due to disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. The utilities sector also managed to eke out a small gain, up 0.2% for the week.

The primary drivers of market volatility this week were geopolitical tensions, particularly the escalating conflict between the US and Iran. News of missile attacks, oil tanker strikes, and disrupted shipping lanes all contributed to a sense of uncertainty and unease among investors. The impact was felt across various sectors, with financials, healthcare, and consumer discretionary stocks among the hardest hit. The financials sector, for example, fell 2.9% on concerns about the potential economic fallout from the conflict. Meanwhile, the consumer discretionary sector dropped 3.3%, likely due to worries about the impact of higher oil prices on consumer spending.

Some of the biggest stories shaping the week's market action included the suspension of oil loading operations in the UAE's Fujairah due to a fire, as well as the US embassy in Iraq being hit by missiles. These events, combined with the ongoing tensions between the US and Iran, led to a surge in oil prices, which in turn benefited the energy sector. Barclays' decision to raise its 2026 Brent forecast to $85 a barrel also reflected the growing concerns about supply disruptions. On the company-specific front, Nvidia was in the news due to rumors of an upcoming AI chip, while Eaton finalized a smart acquisition to advance its AI data center ambitions.

Other sectors that struggled this week included industrials, which fell 3.7%, and communication services, which declined 2.6%. The technology sector also dropped 2.1%, possibly due to concerns about the potential impact of the conflict on global supply chains. In contrast, the energy sector's gain was a notable exception, with companies like ExxonMobil and Chevron likely benefiting from the higher oil prices. As the situation continues to unfold, it will be important to monitor the impact on various sectors and the broader market.

In the bond market, US Treasury yields fell slightly, with the U.S. Aggregate Bond index down 1.2% for the week. The U.S. Treasury index also declined 1.0%, while the Inv Grade Corporate index dropped 2.4%. The High Yield index fell 1.2%, and the Municipal Bond index was down 0.4%. These moves reflect the ongoing search for safe-haven assets amidst the geopolitical uncertainty.
