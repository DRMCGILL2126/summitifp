Weekly Market Recap March 28, 2026

Market Declines Amid Global Tensions Rise

Key Takeaways

  • The Nasdaq suffered the largest decline of the major indices, falling 4.3% for the week, while the S&P 500 Growth index plummeted 5.2% and the S&P 500 Value index dropped 1.0%.
  • The energy sector was a notable exception to the overall market downturn, rising 4.9% due to higher oil prices driven by concerns over supply disruptions in the Middle East.
  • The technology sector was one of the biggest losers, down 5.1%, likely due to concerns over the impact of the conflict on global trade and economic growth, as well as company-specific news such as Meta's legal issues.
  • Investors can expect the ongoing conflict in the Middle East to continue driving market action, with key developments including earnings reports and economic data releases that will provide insight into the global economy.

This week was a challenging one for investors, as major indices across the board saw significant declines. The S&P 500, Dow Jones, and Nasdaq all ended the week in the red, with the Nasdaq taking the biggest hit, down 4.3%. The S&P 500 Growth index was particularly hard hit, falling 5.2%, while the S&P 500 Value index held up relatively better, down only 1.0%.

So, what drove these declines? The ongoing conflict in the Middle East was a major factor, as tensions between the US, Israel, and Iran continued to escalate. News of Yemen's Houthis launching a strike on Israel, as well as reports of US troops being wounded in an Iran strike on a base in Saudi Arabia, weighed heavily on investor sentiment. The energy sector was a notable exception, rising 4.9% on the back of higher oil prices, which were driven in part by concerns over supply disruptions in the region.

The technology sector, on the other hand, was one of the biggest losers of the week, down 5.1%. This was likely due in part to concerns over the impact of the conflict on global trade and economic growth, as well as specific company-related news, such as Meta's legal woes. The communication services sector also struggled, down 5.0%, as investors grew increasingly wary of the potential for further escalation in the region.

Among the other major stories of the week, the US refining group's warning that record biofuel quotas may worsen the Iran war price spike was notable, as was the report that Iranian-linked hackers had breached the FBI director's personal email. These stories, combined with the ongoing geopolitical tensions, helped to drive investor uncertainty and risk aversion.

In terms of specific sector performance, energy and materials led the way, up 4.9% and 2.9%, respectively. Utilities and consumer staples also saw gains, albeit more modest ones, up 1.8% and 0.7%, respectively. On the other hand, financials, consumer discretionary, and technology were among the biggest laggards, down 3.0%, 4.0%, and 5.1%, respectively.

As we look ahead to next week, it's likely that the ongoing conflict in the Middle East will continue to be a major driver of market action. Investors will be watching closely for any developments that could potentially ease or escalate tensions in the region. Additionally, with the first quarter coming to a close, we can expect to see a flurry of earnings reports and economic data releases, which will provide further insight into the health of the global economy.
