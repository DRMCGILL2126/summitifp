Weekly Market Recap April 18, 2026

Markets Surge on Positive Geopolitical Developments

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500 rose 3.5% this week, while the Nasdaq led the charge with a 5.1% gain, driven by positive developments in the Iran-US conflict and its impact on global oil supplies.
  • The technology sector was the top performer, rising 6.0%, and consumer discretionary stocks also did well, up 5.7%, as many of these companies are less directly exposed to fluctuations in oil prices and global trade.
  • Energy stocks lagged significantly, falling 3.7%, due to the increased oil supply resulting from the opening of the Strait of Hormuz, which put downward pressure on prices.
  • Investors will be closely monitoring the situation in Iran and its effects on global trade and oil prices, particularly in sectors such as energy, materials, and industrials, for potential future market impacts.

This week, markets experienced a notable uptick, with the S&P 500 rising 3.5% and the Nasdaq leading the charge with a 5.1% gain. The Dow Jones and other major indices also saw significant increases, with the Russell 2000 up 4.0% and the S&P MidCap 400 up 2.4%. This broad-based rally was largely driven by positive developments in the ongoing Iran-US conflict, particularly the announcement that the Strait of Hormuz is "completely open," which helped to alleviate concerns about global oil supplies and trade disruptions.

The news out of Iran and its impact on global trade and oil prices was a major theme this week, with many headlines focusing on the conflict and its effects on various sectors. The technology sector was a top performer, rising 6.0%, likely due to the fact that many tech companies are less directly exposed to the fluctuations in oil prices and global trade. Consumer discretionary stocks also did well, up 5.7%, possibly as a result of the perceived decrease in geopolitical risk. On the other hand, energy stocks lagged significantly, falling 3.7% as the opening of the Strait of Hormuz and the resulting increase in oil supply put downward pressure on prices.

Among the many news stories that shaped the week's market action, a few stood out as particularly influential. The announcement that the Strait of Hormuz is open, and the subsequent reaction from the shipping industry and global markets, was a major driver of the rally. Additionally, the US decision to renew a waiver on Russian oil imports and the EU's push for jet fuel diversification in response to the Iran war were also significant. These stories not only affected the broader market but also had an impact on specific sectors, such as energy and materials, which were influenced by the changing dynamics of global trade and commodity prices.

Company-specific news was relatively quiet this week, but there were some notable moves in response to the broader market trends. For example, some of the megacap tech stocks, which are often seen as safe havens during times of uncertainty, saw significant gains. Additionally, the financial sector, which had been under pressure due to concerns about the impact of the Iran war on the global economy, saw a modest increase of 1.5% as the market began to price in a more positive outcome.

As we look ahead to next week, it will be important to continue monitoring the situation in Iran and the resulting effects on global trade and oil prices. Any further developments or changes in the conflict could have a significant impact on the market, particularly in sectors such as energy, materials, and industrials. Additionally, investors will be watching for any signs of how the conflict may be affecting the broader economy, including any potential impacts on inflation, interest rates, or consumer spending.
