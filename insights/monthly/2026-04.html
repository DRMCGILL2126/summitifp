Monthly Market Review April 2026

Markets End Month on Turbulent Note

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500 and Nasdaq declined by 1.0% and 1.8% respectively in the given month, while the Dow Jones limited its losses to 0.2% and the MSCI EAFE saw a 0.5% increase.
  • The Iran war was a major driver of market sentiment, causing disruptions to global supply chains and leading to soaring factory input costs worldwide, with sectors like Industrials and Technology declining by 2.0% and 2.8% respectively.
  • Utilities and Real Estate led the sector performance with gains of 1.4% each, while Consumer Discretionary and Industrials were among the laggards, declining by 1.6% and 2.0% respectively.
  • The dollar fell for the second day in a row as Middle East ceasefire expectations rose, while gold neared a two-week high, highlighting the complex interplay between different asset classes in times of uncertainty.

As we closed out the month, it's clear that markets experienced a bit of a tumultuous ride. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq led the decline, down 1.0% and 1.8% respectively, while the Dow Jones managed to limit its losses to just 0.2%. On the other hand, international markets, as represented by the MSCI EAFE, actually saw a slight increase of 0.5%. This divergence in performance is a great example of how different regions and sectors can react to the same news in varying ways.

Speaking of news, it's hard to ignore the overwhelming presence of the Iran war in this month's headlines. From Trump's statements on potentially exiting the war to the various reports of attacks and retaliations, it's clear that this conflict has been a major driver of market sentiment. One of the most significant effects of the war has been the disruption to global supply chains, which has led to soaring factory input costs worldwide. This, in turn, has contributed to the underperformance of sectors like Industrials and Technology, which saw declines of 2.0% and 2.8% respectively.

Top Stories of the Month

In my view, there were three key stories that shaped the month's market action. Firstly, the ongoing Iran war and its potential implications for global stability and trade. Secondly, the comments from Trump and other world leaders on the potential for a ceasefire or de-escalation, which led to bouts of optimism in the markets. Lastly, the reports of rising food and fuel prices, particularly in the UK, which highlighted the war's impact on everyday consumers. These stories all contributed to the volatility we saw in the markets, as investors tried to gauge the potential effects of the conflict on the global economy.

Sector Performance

On a sector level, it was a mixed bag. Utilities and Real Estate led the way, with gains of 1.4% each, likely due to their perceived safe-haven status during times of uncertainty. Energy and Materials also saw gains, up 1.1% each, potentially benefiting from the increased geopolitical tensions and the resulting commodity price increases. On the other hand, Consumer Discretionary and Industrials were among the laggards, with declines of 1.6% and 2.0% respectively. The underperformance of these sectors can be tied to the rising input costs and supply chain disruptions caused by the war.

The news also had a significant impact on the currency and commodity markets. The dollar fell for the second day in a row as Middle East ceasefire expectations rose, while gold neared a two-week high as the dollar dropped. These movements highlight the complex interplay between different asset classes and the ongoing search for safe havens in times of uncertainty.

In terms of broader macro themes, it's clear that the Iran war has become a major driver of market sentiment. The potential for de-escalation or a ceasefire is being closely watched by investors, and any developments on this front are likely to have a significant impact on the markets. Additionally, the rising input costs and supply chain disruptions caused by the war will be an important factor to watch in the coming months, as they have the potential to affect a wide range of sectors and industries.
